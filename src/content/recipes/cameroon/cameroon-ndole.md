Ndole is a slow-built stew centred on bitter leaves — the same plant the dish is named after — cooked down with a peanut and broth sauce, chunks of stewing beef, and finished with king prawns fried in red palm oil. It is the national dish of Cameroon and it rewards patience.

## Instructions

### Prep

1. **Onions:** halve one (for the beef pot), roughly chop one (for the blender), thinly slice one (for the prawns). Keep separate.
2. **Garlic and ginger:** peel — no need to crush or grate, they go straight into the blender.
3. **Scotch bonnet:** deseed and set aside.
4. **Spinach:** roughly chop in batches.

### Cook the Beef

5. Transfer the stewing beef to a pot. Add the halved onion, 2 cloves of garlic, and 2 stock cubes. Cover with ~1 litre of water.
6. Bring to a simmer and cook for **40–50 minutes** until the beef is fall-apart tender.
7. Remove the beef and set aside. **Reserve all the broth.**

### Cook the Peanuts

8. While the beef cooks, place the peanuts in a separate pot and cover with water. Bring to a boil and cook for **10 minutes**. Drain and leave to cool slightly.

### Blend the Sauce

9. Add the roughly chopped onion, remaining 4 garlic cloves, ginger, cooled peanuts, and 1 cup of beef broth to a blender. Blend on high until smooth and creamy. Add more broth if it struggles.

### Build the Ndole

10. Pour the blended sauce into a large pan. Add another cup of beef broth, the ground crayfish, scotch bonnet, 2 more stock cubes, and the cooked beef. Stir to combine.
11. Simmer for **10 minutes**, stirring frequently to prevent burning.
12. Add the bitter leaves and spinach gradually, allowing them to wilt and fit in the pot. Cook for a further **15 minutes**, stirring frequently.

### Fry the Prawns

13. In a separate pan, heat the red palm oil over medium heat. Add the thinly sliced onion and cook for **6–8 minutes** until softening.
14. Add the king prawns and cook for **2–3 minutes**. Try not to stir too much — you want some colour. Reserve a few prawns for plating.
15. Tip everything — prawns, onion, oil and all — into the ndole. The palm oil gives it a glossy finish.

### Boiled Plantain

16. Peel and halve the plantain. Transfer to a pot of salted water and boil for **~20 minutes** until soft.

### Dodo (Fried Plantain)

17. Peel the plantain and slice on the diagonal (the angle increases surface area for better frying).
18. Fry in neutral oil over medium heat for **~3 minutes per side** until golden brown on both sides. Drain on paper towel.

### Plate

19. Spoon the ndole into bowls. Add boiled plantain and dodo on the side. Top with the reserved prawns. Season with salt if needed.

## Verdict

The bitter leaves were appropriately named. This is one of the most bitter things you'll eat — whether that's down to a particularly strong batch or just the nature of the dish is hard to say. The peanut sauce is rich and well-built, the prawns are excellent, and the dodo is genuinely delicious. But the bitterness dominates everything else. Proceed with awareness.
